## Key Question 1: Which parser(s) should be the default for engineering PDFs, and when is a vision LLM worth the cost?

### Takeaway
As of late 2026 the open benchmarks have split document parsing into two tiers: classic layout-model pipelines (Docling, Marker, MinerU-pipeline, Unstructured, PyMuPDF) that run on CPU at roughly 0.3-3 pages/s but score 50-86 on OmniDocBench/olmOCR-bench, and small "expert VLM" parsers (MinerU2.5 1.2B, PaddleOCR-VL, olmOCR-2, dots.ocr) that need a GPU but score 90-96 and beat GPT-5.2 and match Gemini 3 on tables and formulas. For engineering PDFs the pragmatic default is a GPU-hosted expert VLM (MinerU2.5/PaddleOCR-VL, or Marker balanced mode) for anything with tables, formulas or scans, with a cheap text-layer pass (PyMuPDF/Docling fast) for born-digital prose; frontier general VLMs (Gemini 3, Claude, GPT-5.x) are only worth paying for on pages the pipeline flags as low-confidence, on diagrams/P&IDs, and for semantic extraction (not plain transcription), because on pure parsing they no longer beat the sub-2B open models.

### Inferences
- Default stack recommendation for engineering PDFs (inference from the benchmark pattern): (a) MinerU2.5 (1.2B VLM via vLLM) or PaddleOCR-VL as the primary parser on a single GPU, because they lead OmniDocBench on table TEDS (87-95) and formula CDM (96-97), beat GPT-5.2 and Qwen3-VL-235B, and have permissive-enough licences for internal use; (b) Docling as the "glue" layer for DOCX/XLSX/PPTX and for its MIT licence, DoclingDocument schema and hierarchical chunker; (c) Marker if the organisation qualifies under the <$5M weights licence or buys a licence, since it is the fastest accurate pipeline (2.9 pg/s balanced on a big GPU host). Teams must run their own 50-100 page sample because every published number comes from the vendor or benchmark owner.
- Frontier general VLMs (Gemini 3, Claude, GPT-5.x) are no longer worth paying for pure page transcription: Gemini 3 Pro (92.91) trails MinerU2.5-Pro (95.75) and PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 (96.34), and GPT-5.2 (86.59) barely matches MinerU-pipeline. They are worth it for (1) pages the pipeline flags as low-confidence (Marker's `--use_llm` pattern), (2) reading diagrams, P&IDs, charts and photos where there is no text layer to transcribe, (3) semantic extraction/classification where the output is structured fields, and (4) cross-page table merging. Hallucinated field values are the documented risk, so pair VLM extraction with a "value must appear in OCR text" check.
- Nougat (Meta, 2023) is effectively superseded: it is not on the current OmniDocBench leaderboard and no 2025-2026 source in this research recommends it; its niche (academic PDFs with LaTeX math) is covered by MinerU2.5/Marker formula handling.
- For P&IDs and drawings, text parsers are the wrong tool: the evidence favours (1) symbol/tag detection with vision models plus deterministic rule/graph stages, (2) DEXPI/DXF structured data where available, and (3) VLM only for tag reading and title-block fields with human verification.
- Cloud APIs are clearly superior only in two situations: when you cannot run a GPU (Mistral OCR 3 at $1-2/1,000 pages is the cheapest hosted parser with decent accuracy, although it scores below the open VLMs on olmOCR-bench), and when you need managed compliance, SLAs and forms/key-value prebuilt models (Azure DI Layout at ~$10/1,000 pages). For table-heavy pages, Reducto/Pulse-class vendors score 0.93-0.97 on RD-TableBench vs 0.85 for Azure/Textract but cost ~10x more per page.

### Gaps
- No independent, like-for-like pages/sec comparison of MinerU2.5 VLM backend vs Marker vs Docling on the same CPU and GPU; MinerU publishes no throughput figure, and olmOCR publishes only $/M pages.
- OmniDocBench does not list Docling, Unstructured, LlamaParse, Reducto, Azure DI, Textract, Google Document AI, Mistral OCR or Claude, so cross-family accuracy comparison relies on vendor benchmarks (RD-TableBench, olmOCR-bench) with different metrics.
- No benchmark specific to engineering content (requirement clauses, units, datasheets, multi-column standards) was found; OmniDocBench is dominated by academic papers, books, slides, financial reports.
- Azure DI page-dimension limits for large-format drawings could not be verified (learn.microsoft.com unreachable); only Google's 40 MP per image cap was confirmed.
- Chunkr pricing, Unstructured pages/sec, and Granite-Docling 258M accuracy figures were not found.

## Key Question 2: What chunking and indexing approach gives the best QA accuracy on specifications and standards?

### Takeaway
The evidence from 2024-2026 converges on structure-aware chunking (split on the document's own heading/clause/table hierarchy, keep tables atomic, cap chunks around 400-800 tokens) combined with Anthropic-style contextual prefixes, hybrid BM25+dense retrieval and a reranker; this stack cut top-20 retrieval failures by 67% in Anthropic's tests and structure-aware chunking won on oil-and-gas enterprise documents at lower compute cost than semantic chunking. Breakpoint "semantic" chunking repeatedly underperforms end-to-end, and chunking choice matters as much as embedding-model choice.

### Cited Findings
- Anthropic Contextual Retrieval (Sept 2024): prepending a 50-100 token LLM-generated context to each chunk before embedding/BM25 reduced top-20 retrieval failure rate from 5.7% to 3.7% (contextual embeddings alone, -35%), to 2.9% with contextual BM25 (-49%), and to 1.9% with reranking (-67%); one-time cost with prompt caching $1.02 per million document tokens (800-token chunks, 8k-token docs); passing 20 chunks beat 5 or 10; recommended retrieving ~150 then reranking to 20; knowledge bases under ~200k tokens (~500 pages) can skip RAG entirely; Gemini and Voyage embeddings performed particularly well — [Anthropic Contextual Retrieval](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)
- Vectara (NAACL 2025, arXiv 2410.13070) tested 25 chunking configurations against 48 embedding models and found chunking configuration had as much or more influence on retrieval quality as embedding model choice — reported via [Prem AI 2026 chunking guide](https://blog.premai.io/rag-chunking-strategies-the-2026-benchmark-guide) and [Tianpan blog](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-20-chunking-strategy-rag-quality) (snippet; primary paper not fetched)
- Oil-and-gas enterprise documents (arXiv 2603.24556, 2026): compared fixed-size sliding window, recursive, breakpoint semantic, and structure-aware chunking; structure-aware gave higher overall retrieval effectiveness, especially top-K metrics, with "significantly lower computational costs than semantic or baseline strategies"; on P&IDs all strategies scored low (structure-aware MRR 0.106 vs 0.070) — [arXiv 2603.24556](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.24556) (snippet only)
- A 2026 study evaluated 36 segmentation methods (fixed, semantic, structure-aware, hierarchical, adaptive, LLM-assisted) across six domains with five embedding models using nDCG@5, Hit@5, MRR — [arXiv 2603.06976](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.06976) (snippet only; rankings not retrieved)
- Blog-reported benchmark summaries: recursive splitting at 400-512 tokens with 10-20% overlap gave 85-90% Recall@5 with lowest variance (attributed to a Chroma 2024 study); semantic chunking had high isolated recall but ~54% end-to-end accuracy, ~15 points behind recursive, because fragments were too thin; a "context cliff" near 2,500 tokens where answer quality drops — [Prem AI guide](https://blog.premai.io/rag-chunking-strategies-the-2026-benchmark-guide); [Denser.ai comparison](https://denser.ai/blog/rag-chunking-strategies/) (vendor blogs, unverified)
- Parent-child (small-to-big) retrieval pattern: index ~100-300 token children, return the ~1,500 token parent; ~5-15 children per parent; operational cost is multiple index levels and a routing layer; Amazon Bedrock Knowledge Bases offers hierarchical chunking natively — [ZeroEntropy concept page](https://www.zeroentropy.dev/concepts/parent-document-retrieval/); [reintech guide](https://reintech.io/blog/implement-parent-child-document-retrieval-rag-systems); no head-to-head numbers were found
- Standards-document chunking in practice: SSKG Hub (LLM knowledge graphs from sustainability standards) uses 4,000-character chunks with 200-character overlap, strict triple output and a prohibition on unsupported inference — [SSKG Hub, arXiv 2603.00669](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.00669) (snippet only)
- Requirement-level extraction: ReqInOne has the LLM emit each requirement in an INCOSE-derived pattern (subject, action, object, qualifier, condition) and append the source and reason for each extraction — [ReqInOne, arXiv 2508.09648](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.09648) (snippet only); a 2026 automotive study reports 98.2% extraction accuracy with full traceability over four specification standards — [arXiv 2603.20534](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20534v2) (snippet only); a MoDRE 2026 ARINC-653 evaluation found few-shot prompting beat few-shot+RAG — [MoDRE 2026 slides](https://www.modre2026.ece.mcgill.ca/proceedings/MoDRE2026_4_presentation.pdf) (snippet only)
- A legal-RAG write-up reports retrieval missing a clause split across a chunk boundary, arguing chunking should follow clause structure — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/alaikrm/when-rag-gives-wrong-answers-a-debugging-walkthrough-2bn8)
- Table-aware chunking exists in tooling: krishddd/llm-wiki does "layout-aware chunking that keeps tables and images atomic" and an "agentic chunk plan" that adapts chunk size to content density — [krishddd/llm-wiki](https://github.com/krishddd/llm-wiki); LightRAG offers four chunking strategies and parses Word/Markdown tables and formulas natively — [HKUDS/LightRAG](https://github.com/HKUDS/LightRAG)
- Multi-page/multi-document dependency chunking with VLMs is an active research direction (M3DocDep) — [arXiv 2605.18774](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18774) (title only)

### Inferences
- For specifications and standards, the best-supported recipe is: parse to a structured document model (Docling/MinerU JSON with heading levels and table cells) -> chunk at the numbered clause level (e.g., "7.3.2") so each "shall" statement is retrievable with its clause ID, with parent chunks at the section level for context -> keep every table as one atomic chunk (serialised as markdown or HTML, with the caption and the heading path) -> prepend a generated context line (document title/number, revision, section path, 1-sentence summary) per Anthropic's method -> index in both BM25 and a dense model -> rerank. Clause IDs and tag numbers are exact-match tokens, which is why BM25 matters as much as embeddings for engineering text.
- Breakpoint semantic chunking should be avoided for standards: it is costlier and the oil-and-gas study plus multiple practitioner benchmarks show it loses to structure-aware and even recursive splitting end to end.
- P&IDs need a separate index: parse tags/symbols to a graph (DEXPI or extracted), store the drawing as an image for VLM question answering, and embed only textual tag lists and title-block metadata. Text chunking of P&IDs is near-useless per the 2026 study.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study comparing requirement-clause-level chunking against section-level chunking on standards QA was found.
- Rankings from the 36-method segmentation study (arXiv 2603.06976) and full tables from the oil-and-gas study could not be retrieved (arxiv.org unreachable).
- No measured accuracy gain for parent-child retrieval on technical documents was found; the pattern is widely recommended but evidence is anecdotal.

## Key Question 3: What is the proven pattern for an LLM maintaining a wiki incrementally from a growing corpus, with human approval, and which projects demonstrate it?

### Takeaway
The dominant 2026 pattern is Karpathy's "LLM wiki": immutable raw sources, an LLM-owned markdown wiki (entity/concept/summary pages plus index.md and log.md), and a schema file, operated via ingest / query / lint, with the LLM revising 10-15 existing pages per new source and flagging contradictions. Production-grade implementations (nashsu/llm_wiki at ~20.8k stars, krishddd/llm-wiki) add SHA-256 change detection, staged edits, confidence-gated human review queues and bi-temporal facts; STORM/Co-STORM demonstrate cited encyclopedia-article synthesis but are pre-writing aids, while GraphRAG community reports and LightRAG/Cognee provide graph-level summaries with incremental updates and source traceability but not editable wiki pages.

### Cited Findings

**Karpathy LLM wiki (idea file, April 2026)**
- Three layers: raw sources (immutable, "source of truth"), the wiki ("The LLM owns this layer entirely": summaries, entity and concept pages, comparisons, overview, synthesis), and the schema (CLAUDE.md/AGENTS.md defining structure and workflows, co-evolved by human and LLM) — [Karpathy llm-wiki gist (fork)](https://gist.github.com/KennyKang7012/1e0e366fa2080f823a54d3618607783d)
- Ingest: read source, write summary page, update index, revise relevant entity/concept pages, append log entry; "a single source may touch 10-15 pages"; the author prefers one source at a time with close supervision; the LLM notes "where new data contradicts old claims" — same source
- Query: find pages, synthesise an answer "with citations"; good answers can be filed back as new wiki pages. Lint: periodic checks for contradictions, stale claims "that newer sources have superseded", orphan pages, missing concept pages, missing cross-references — same source
- index.md is the catalog read first on every query; log.md is append-only with entries like `## [2026-04-02] ingest | Article Title`; index-only navigation works "surprisingly well" to ~100 sources and hundreds of pages, beyond which a search tool (qmd: hybrid BM25/vector with LLM re-ranking, MCP server) is suggested — same source
- The gist earned 5,000+ stars and spawned 241+ community implementations — [LLM wiki explained](https://srisatyalokesh.is-a.dev/learn-ai/llm-wiki-explained-karpathy-pattern/) (secondary)
- An August 2026 write-up claims the pattern works for personal knowledge but that six months in production surfaced three failure modes Karpathy does not address — [MPT Solutions tag page](https://www.mpt.solutions/tag/knowledge-management/) (snippet only; failure modes not retrieved)
- Obsidian community plugin "Karpathy LLM Wiki" (Greener-Dalii) implements ingest/query/lint inside Obsidian with multiple providers incl. local Ollama — [Obsidian plugin listing](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/karpathywiki); [GitHub](https://github.com/green-dalii/obsidian-llm-wiki)

**nashsu/llm_wiki (desktop app, GPL-3.0, ~20.8k stars / 2.4k forks)**
- Layers: `raw/sources/`, `wiki/` (entities, concepts, source summaries, queries, index.md, log.md, overview.md), `schema.md` plus `purpose.md` — [nashsu/llm_wiki](https://github.com/nashsu/llm_wiki)
- Ingest = two sequential LLM calls: (1) analyse source incl. connections to existing wiki content and "contradictions & tensions"; (2) generate pages, cross-references and review items. Files SHA-256 hashed, unchanged skipped; persistent queue with 3 retries; folder watcher for adds/edits/deletes; deleting a source cascades to its summary page, updates shared pages, cleans index and dead wikilinks — same source
- Provenance: every page has `sources: []` frontmatter linking raw inputs; chat answers cite pages by number with a references panel; "Read Sources Only" mode answers only from imported material — same source
- Human review: ingest flags items needing judgment; users resolve asynchronously with constrained actions (Create Page, Deep Research, Skip) without blocking ingest; local API exposes review items — same source
- Formats: PDF (built-in parser, optional MinerU cloud/local), DOCX, PPTX, XLSX/XLS/ODS, EPUB/MOBI, Org, images, audio/video, web clips; providers OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Ollama, OpenAI-compatible — same source

**krishddd/llm-wiki (FastAPI service, MIT)**
- SQLite knowledge graph + BM25 + dense index (Chroma); loaders for PDF, DOCX, PPTX, XLSX, HTML, MD, TXT with structure-aware parsing and layout-aware chunking keeping tables/images atomic; Doc2Query question generation — [krishddd/llm-wiki](https://github.com/krishddd/llm-wiki)
- Incremental merge: a reconciler edits existing pages overlapping a new source on >=2 entities; each edit labelled refining / contradicting / adding context; edits staged in `wiki/review/edits/`; refine and contradict edits auto-apply only when the old text still matches — same source
- Bi-temporal facts: `ingested_at`, `valid_from`, `valid_to`, `superseded_by`, `last_reinforced`, `access_count`; "a new source never deletes an old fact" but sets `valid_to`/`superseded_by`; confidence decays with a 90-day half-life unless reinforced — same source
- Contradictions: detector compares new claims to related pages; auto-resolver keeps the winner when composite-score margin >=0.2, else queued at `GET /admin/contradictions` for humans — same source
- Human approval: pages scoring <0.60 at ingest go to `wiki/review/` instead of live; Review Autopilot re-reads the source and scores faithfulness/coverage/entity grounding, auto-accepts >=0.70, auto-archives <=0.30, humans decide the middle via `POST /review/{id}/accept|reject`; rejection archives rather than deletes; claim-verification pass checks each cited claim against its snippet; episodic and audit logs with correlation IDs; weekly lint autofix — same source

**STORM / Co-STORM (Stanford OVAL, MIT licence)**
- Two stages: pre-writing (perspective-guided question asking, simulated writer-expert conversations grounded in retrieved sources, outline) and writing (cited full-length article), four customisable modules (knowledge curation, outline, article generation, polishing); retrievers include VectorRM for grounding on the user's own documents, plus Bing/You/Serper/Brave/SearXNG/Tavily/Google/AzureAISearch; any litellm model; built on dspy; `pip install knowledge-storm` — [stanford-oval/storm](https://github.com/stanford-oval/storm)
- Co-STORM adds LLM expert agents, a moderator that asks about retrieved-but-unused information, a turn-management discourse protocol, and a dynamic hierarchical mind map; humans observe or inject utterances — same source
- Papers: STORM NAACL 2024; Co-STORM EMNLP 2024; FreshWiki and WildSeek datasets; 70,000+ users of the preview; authors state it "cannot produce publication-ready articles" and output needs significant editing — same source

**Microsoft GraphRAG**
- Index: LLM extracts entities and relationships over the whole corpus into a knowledge graph; bottom-up hierarchical clustering partitions it into semantic clusters; the LLM pre-generates a report per cluster; whole-dataset questions are answered from the cluster reports; relationships are traced to specific source snippets and reports/relationships are cited by ID so humans can audit each assertion — [Microsoft Research GraphRAG blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/graphrag-unlocking-llm-discovery-on-narrative-private-data/)
- Evaluation: LLM-graded pairwise wins over baseline RAG on comprehensiveness, human enfranchisement and diversity; faithfulness via SelfCheckGPT "similar" to baseline; no numeric win rates on the blog — same source
- GraphRAG 1.0 added `graphrag update`, which "computes the deltas between an existing index and newly added content and intelligently merges the updates to minimize re-indexing", because new content can alter community structure so much that much of an index must be recomputed; caching makes re-runs cheaper — [Moving to GraphRAG 1.0](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/moving-to-graphrag-1-0-streamlining-ergonomics-for-developers-and-users/); the update command has `standard` (LLM) and `fast` (NLP) methods and writes `community_reports.parquet` to `update_output/` — [GraphRAG CLI docs mirror](https://www.mintlify.com/microsoft/graphrag/cli/update)
- The repo is MIT and "largely in maintenance mode, and won't be accepting new PRs or implementing new features" (bug/CVE fixes only); indexing "can be expensive, so start small" — [microsoft/graphrag README](https://github.com/microsoft/graphrag); the Azure Solution Accelerator is archived and does not support incremental indexing — [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5578950/how-do-i-add-new-documents-to-my-microsoft-graph-r)

**LightRAG (HKUDS, MIT, ~40.2k stars)**
- Dual-layer KG + vector; incremental insert without full rebuild (paper); document deletion with automatic KG regeneration (Aug 2025) reusing the LLM cache; entities/relations linked to source chunks and file paths (`MAX_FILE_PATHS`, `MAX_SOURCE_IDS_PER_RELATION`); citation functionality for source attribution (Mar 2025); API returns retrieved contexts (Nov 2025); RAG-Anything merged May 2026 adding multimodal parsing via MinerU and Docling services; default in-memory storage "not for production", PostgreSQL recommended — [HKUDS/LightRAG](https://github.com/HKUDS/LightRAG); [LightRAG paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05779v3)

**Cognee (Apache-2.0, ~32.6k stars)**
- Current API: `remember`, `recall`, `improve`, `forget`; Postgres (PGVector) can hold metadata, vectors and graph state; v1.6.1 stamps document `external_metadata` on every chunk and surfaces it in hybrid retrieval; v1.6.0 pipeline recovery preserves completed documents after crashes; self-host via Docker (API 8000, UI 3000, MCP 8001); BEAM self-reported scores 0.79 at 100K tokens and 0.67 at 10M tokens — [topoteretes/cognee](https://github.com/topoteretes/cognee); a Cognee-published benchmark compares it to Mem0, Graphiti, LightRAG on HotPotQA/TwoWikiMultiHop/MuSiQue — [Cognee blog](https://www.cognee.ai/blog/deep-dives?page=3)

### Inferences
- The proven pattern for an engineering corpus is a hybrid: Karpathy's three-layer wiki for the human-readable, editable knowledge layer, with (a) a parsed-document store and hybrid search index underneath (the gist itself says index-only breaks past ~100 sources), (b) a GraphRAG/LightRAG-style entity graph for equipment tags, standards and systems so entity pages can be regenerated from structured facts, and (c) krishddd-style staged edits and confidence thresholds for approval. nashsu/llm_wiki and krishddd/llm-wiki are the two most complete open demonstrations; neither is engineering-specific.
- "Human approval" in practice means: ingest never writes live pages directly for low-confidence or contradicting edits; it writes proposals (diffs) to a review queue; auto-apply only for edits whose anchor text still matches; everything logged. Git on the wiki folder (mentioned by Karpathy) gives free diff review and rollback.
- STORM is useful as the article-synthesis engine for first-draft topic pages (e.g., "Centrifugal pump seal selection per API 682") when pointed at the internal corpus via VectorRM, but its own authors say output requires substantial editing, so it fits the "draft -> review" slot, not autonomous publishing.
- GraphRAG community reports are a model for auto-generated "overview" pages (system-level or discipline-level summaries) but GraphRAG's maintenance-mode status and index cost suggest using LightRAG or a custom extraction for the graph layer.

### Gaps
- The "three production failure modes" of the LLM-wiki pattern (Aug 2026 article) could not be retrieved; the original Hacker News thread and Karpathy's own gist URL were not reachable (only a fork was fetched).
- No peer-reviewed evaluation of wiki-style compiled knowledge vs RAG on question accuracy was found.
- GraphRAG's exact mechanics for regenerating community reports during `update` are undocumented in the sources reached.

## Key Question 4: How should revisions of the same document (Rev A, Rev B) be handled so the wiki and answers reflect the current revision?

### Takeaway
Treat document identity and revision as separate keys, index every revision but gate default retrieval to the "active" revision via metadata filters, keep superseded revisions queryable for history and change comparison, and, in the wiki layer, supersede rather than delete facts (bi-temporal `valid_from/valid_to/superseded_by`), regenerating affected pages when a new revision lands. VersionRAG (2025) formalises this and reports large gains over naive RAG on version-sensitive questions.

### Cited Findings
- VersionRAG (arXiv 2510.08109) frames version-aware retrieval as underexplored, argues "even minor version changes can fundamentally alter the validity of an answer", and reports large gains over naive RAG on version-sensitive questions — [VersionRAG](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.08109) (snippet only; numbers not retrieved)
- Practitioner design: separate a document's stable identity from its revision; each chunk carries a revision state (active, superseded, draft, archived); current-state queries filter to active before similarity scoring; historical queries widen the filter; superseded content is retired from default retrieval, not deleted, because audits and change comparisons need it; build and verify a new revision before atomically moving the active pointer so a failed ingest never leaves a mixed old/new state — [ZimaSpace: reliable version selection](https://shop.zimaspace.com/blogs/tech-ai-hub/features-reliable-document-version-selection-rag); [ZimaSpace: versioning keeps answers current](https://shop.zimaspace.com/blogs/tech-ai-hub/document-versioning-keeps-rag-answers-current-files)
- Citation drift: citations point to old revisions when re-chunking or stale index entries break the chunk-to-current-source link; "the most relevant chunk is not necessarily the most authoritative" because an old revision can match a query as well as the new one — [ZimaSpace: superseded citations](https://shop.zimaspace.com/en-ca/blogs/tech-ai-hub/rag-citations-point-superseded-document-versions)
- Wiki-layer precedent: krishddd/llm-wiki records `valid_from`, `valid_to`, `superseded_by` per fact, never deletes old facts, and its contradiction detector supersedes older facts on concrete conflict — [krishddd/llm-wiki](https://github.com/krishddd/llm-wiki); Karpathy's lint step targets "stale claims that newer sources have superseded" — [Karpathy gist (fork)](https://gist.github.com/KennyKang7012/1e0e366fa2080f823a54d3618607783d); nashsu/llm_wiki cascades source deletion to summary pages, shared pages, index and wikilinks — [nashsu/llm_wiki](https://github.com/nashsu/llm_wiki)
- Graph-layer precedent: LightRAG regenerates affected entities/relationships on document deletion using the cached LLM outputs — [HKUDS/LightRAG](https://github.com/HKUDS/LightRAG); GraphRAG `update` computes deltas and merges — [GraphRAG 1.0 blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/moving-to-graphrag-1-0-streamlining-ergonomics-for-developers-and-users/)
- Revision/status conventions: under ISO 19650 each published container has a unique human-readable ID built from an agreed field convention with delimiters; status code (e.g., S4 = published for stage approval) states permitted use and revision code (e.g., P02 = provisional, second issue) shows position in the publication sequence; the exact string is project-defined — [NBS ISO 19650 support article](https://support.thenbs.com/support/solutions/articles/7000055103); the Irish HSE national-annex convention defines fields with max lengths and notes revision "O" was changed to "0" — [HSE NEIS naming convention](https://www.hse.ie/eng/about/who/healthbusinessservices/estates/neis-file-naming-convention.pdf); Autodesk Docs treats status and revision as attributes rather than core filename fields — [Autodesk naming standard](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/Docs-Files/files/files-naming-standard/Set_Up_Naming_Standard.html)
- EDMS vendors describe revision codes as standard or user-defined with automatic updates triggered by workflow status, and warn that a separate register spreadsheet drifts from the files ("two versions of reality") — [Proarc EDMS listing](https://apps.microsoft.com/detail/9pc2x1whtvx2?hl=en-US); [Folderit engineering document control](https://www.folderit.com/blog/engineering-document-control-software-drawings-versions-and-approvals/)

### Inferences
- Concrete design: `document_id` (doc number without revision) + `revision` + `status` + `effective_date` as mandatory chunk and page metadata; a `current_revision` pointer per document_id; default retriever filter `revision == current`; a "what changed between Rev A and Rev B" tool that diffs the two parsed documents (structured diff of clauses/tables) and feeds the diff, not the whole document, to the wiki-update step so only affected entity/topic pages are revised. Wiki pages should cite `DOC-123 Rev B §7.3.2 p.14`, and lint should flag any page still citing a non-current revision.
- Revision detection should combine filename convention parsing (ISO 19650-style fields), title-block/cover-page extraction (VLM or template) and a near-duplicate check (hash of parsed text plus embedding similarity) to catch re-issued documents whose filename does not follow convention.
- Because similarity ranking cannot tell authoritative from stale, "current" must be enforced by metadata filtering, not by hoping the reranker prefers newer text.

### Gaps
- VersionRAG's numeric results and its handling of partially-revised documents were not retrieved.
- No source documented how engineering EDMS products expose revision metadata to downstream AI pipelines (APIs/fields), nor an evaluation of LLM-generated change summaries between revisions.

## Key Question 5: Realistic ingestion throughput for ~10,000 documents on CPU-only vs with one GPU

### Takeaway
Published figures put classic pipelines at roughly 0.3-1.3 pages/s per CPU process for born-digital PDFs (far less with OCR) and 2-9 pages/s on a single mid-range GPU, while text-layer-only extraction runs at tens to thousands of pages/s; so a 10,000-document corpus of ~300k pages is a multi-day CPU job but roughly one GPU-day with a modern parser, and hosted VLM OCR for the same corpus costs on the order of $60-600 depending on vendor.

### Cited Findings
- Docling: median 0.79 s/page (x86 CPU), 0.32 s/page (M3 Max), 114 ms/page (L4 GPU); averages 3.1 s, 1.26 s and 481 ms respectively; OCR is the most expensive component — [Docling paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.17887); ~7.9 pg/s on RTX 5090 and 4.2 pg/s on RTX 5070 without OCR, 1.2-1.5 pg/s CPU with 16 threads — [Docling GPU docs mirror](https://www.mintlify.com/docling-project/docling/advanced/gpu-acceleration)
- Datalab run on one B200 host (sustained concurrent): Marker balanced 2.9 pg/s, Marker fast 7.4 pg/s, MinerU pipeline 0.54 pg/s, Docling 2.1 pg/s; CPU: Marker fast no-OCR 23.7 pg/s, liteparse no-OCR 1,721 pg/s; single-stream balanced ~0.3 pg/s — [datalab-to/marker README](https://github.com/datalab-to/marker)
- olmOCR-2: needs >=12 GB VRAM GPU; <$200 per million pages; hosted token prices $0.07-0.10 input / $0.15-0.20 output per M tokens — [allenai/olmocr README](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr); third-party estimate ~$108/M pages hosted — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/andrew-ooo/olmocr-review-allenais-vlm-beats-mistral-marker-on-pdfs-4cci)
- MinerU: GPU tier needs >=8 GB VRAM; CPU tiers exist (ONNX ~2 GB RAM; ONNX+llama.cpp ~8 GB RAM) but no throughput published — [opendatalab/MinerU README](https://github.com/opendatalab/MinerU)
- Hosted per-page prices: Mistral OCR 3 $1-2 per 1,000 pages — [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/01/mistral-ocr3); Azure DI Read $1.50 / Layout $10 per 1,000 — [imagetotable comparison](https://imagetotable.ai/blog/google-vs-aws-vs-azure-ocr-2026); LlamaParse $1.25-56 per 1,000 by tier — [dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/best-ai-document-processing-tools); Reducto ~$15 per 1,000 standard pages — [tooljunction](https://www.tooljunction.io/blog/best-document-parsing-apis-2026)
- Contextual-retrieval enrichment adds ~$1.02 per million document tokens with prompt caching — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)

### Inferences (arithmetic on the cited rates; assumes 10,000 documents averaging 30 pages = 300,000 pages, a mix of born-digital and scanned; adjust linearly)
- CPU-only, one 16-thread server: Docling/Marker-fast class at 0.3-1.3 pg/s effective -> 64-280 hours (2.7-11.6 days) of wall-clock for layout-aware parsing; with OCR on a meaningful scanned fraction, expect the slow end or worse. Text-layer-only extraction (PyMuPDF, Marker `--disable_ocr`, liteparse) finishes in minutes to a few hours but yields poor tables and no scans. Running N CPU workers scales roughly linearly, so a 4-node CPU farm brings the layout-aware pass under 3 days.
- One GPU (L4/RTX 4090/5090 class, 12-24 GB): Docling or Marker balanced at 2-9 pg/s -> 9-42 hours; Marker fast at ~7 pg/s -> ~12 hours; MinerU2.5 VLM via vLLM has no published rate but its 1.2B size suggests throughput in the same single-digit pg/s band on a 24 GB card (unverified); olmOCR-2 7B on a 24 GB card is feasible but slower per page than 1.2B models (unverified).
- Hosted alternatives for 300k pages: olmOCR via DeepInfra/Cirrascale ~$30-60; Mistral OCR 3 batch ~$300 (or ~$600 synchronous); Azure DI Layout ~$3,000; LlamaParse Agentic ~$3,750; Reducto ~$4,500. Contextual-retrieval enrichment for ~150M document tokens (~500 tokens/page) adds ~$150 of LLM cost, and LLM-driven wiki synthesis will dominate cost thereafter (Karpathy: 10-15 page edits per source).
- Plan the pipeline in two passes: fast text-layer pass for all documents on day one (search works immediately), then a GPU VLM pass prioritised by document class (datasheets, standards with tables, scans, drawings) over the following days; this matches Marker's own design (fast mode calls the VLM only where needed).

### Gaps
- No published CPU pages/sec for MinerU or olmOCR (olmOCR is GPU-only); no pages/sec for any parser with OCR enabled on scanned engineering drawings at A1/A0 sizes.
- Datalab's throughput numbers are on a B200 host; per-GPU figures for consumer cards exist only for Docling.

## Key Question 6 (supporting): Engineering-specific metadata extraction — document type, discipline, equipment tags, standards references

### Takeaway
Evidence favours a layered approach: vision/CLIP-style classifiers to separate drawings from documents (~93-94.5% vs ~49% for text-only), template or fuzzy-geometry extraction of title blocks, deterministic parsing of ISO 19650-style filename fields (originator, discipline, type, revision, status), regex plus validation against a master tag list for ISA-5.1 instrument tags, and LLM extraction constrained to verifiable strings for standards references; no benchmark for datasheet-vs-spec-vs-standard sub-typing exists, so a labelled internal set is required.

### Cited Findings
- Drawing vs document classification: CLIP ~93%, CLIP+heuristics ~94.5%, text-only BART-large ~49% with 0.8% drawing recall on a 1,500-file engineering archive test set; authors conclude vision-driven zero-shot methods are highly effective for large-scale filtering — [BLUEPRINT, arXiv 2602.13345](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.13345) (snippet only)
- Text-only prompt-based LLM classification "can limit performance in documents where layout or visual features are critical" — [Document Intelligence survey, arXiv 2510.13366](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.13366) (snippet only)
- Datasheet extraction evaluation: a 2026 manufacturing paper compares human-assisted, layout-aware and zero-shot LLM extraction on technical datasheets and argues common text metrics miss semantic correctness of the resulting structure, proposing a set-based Jaccard index — [Springer J. Intelligent Manufacturing](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10845-026-02894-4) (snippet only)
- ISO 19650 naming: unique ID from agreed delimited fields; status code (S-codes) and revision code (P/C series) carry workflow meaning; projects define their own field set — [NBS support](https://support.thenbs.com/support/solutions/articles/7000055103); national-annex conventions fix field lengths/obligations — [HSE NEIS convention](https://www.hse.ie/eng/about/who/healthbusinessservices/estates/neis-file-naming-convention.pdf); practitioner guides confirm ISO 19650 does not dictate one universal string — [ArchGyan guide](https://archgyan.com/bim-naming-conventions-file-standards/)
- Metadata taxonomy design: classification (hierarchical categories for navigation/retention) is distinct from a metadata taxonomy (controlled vocabularies such as document type, region, confidentiality); ontologies add relationships at higher governance cost — [geosurge taxonomy article](https://geosurge.ai/corpus/gen-3665/document-management-system/ai-ready-document-classification-and-metadata-taxonomy-design-for-modern-dms.html); EDMS scope covers drawings, calculations, equipment datasheets, inspection plans across store/classify/review/approve/revise/distribute/archive — [energy document control guide](https://mintlify.com/sajaldi/energy/guides/document-control); DMS need flexible numbering to map internal, supplier and client conventions — [Ascertra](https://www.ascertra.com/blog/document-control-software)
- Instrument tags: ANSI/ISA-5.1-2009 (reaffirmed 2014) defines a tag as a unique identifier for each instrument/function/device built from a function identifier, loop number and suffix; formats are not dictated by vendor controller naming — [ISA-5.1 tag conventions](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/plc-and-dcs-tag-naming-conventions-isa-51-structure-and-examples); [ISA S5.1 suffix standards](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/scada-tag-naming-conventions-isa-s51-suffix-standards) (secondary blog, not the standard text)
- P&ID tag extraction practice: a Feb 2025 Cognite community thread asks how to extract all tags without predefined patterns and reports multimodal LLMs "have shown promising results" — [Cognite Hub thread](https://hub.cognite.com/product-user-community-428/enhancing-asset-data-quality-automating-p-id-tag-extraction-4620) (snippet only); the 2026 LAPSE paper separates tag extraction from topology inference — [LAPSE 2026.0420](https://psecommunity.org/wp-content/plugins/wpor/includes/file/2606/LAPSE-2026.0420-1v1.pdf); DXF-based compliance checking reached 97.5% F1 vs 55.9% image-LLM — [EC3 2026](https://ec-3.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/EC32026_260.pdf)
- Requirements provenance pattern: have the LLM "append the source of each extracted requirement along with the reason for its extraction" — [ReqInOne](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.09648) (snippet only)
- Metadata propagation in tooling: Cognee v1.6.1 stamps document `external_metadata` on every chunk and surfaces it in hybrid retrieval — [topoteretes/cognee](https://github.com/topoteretes/cognee); LightRAG links entities/relations to file paths and chunk IDs — [HKUDS/LightRAG](https://github.com/HKUDS/LightRAG)

### Inferences
- Document-type classification should be a two-stage router: (1) page-image classifier (CLIP-class or small VLM) for drawing / form / text / table-heavy / scan, (2) LLM classification on first 2 pages' text plus title block for spec / datasheet / standard / report / procedure / drawing register, with the filename's ISO 19650-style type and discipline codes used as strong priors. Discipline tagging (fluid/mechanical/electrical/civil/structural/chemical/materials) can similarly use the discipline field when present and an LLM multi-label pass over headings and standards cited (e.g., API/ASME B31.3 -> piping/mechanical; IEC 60079 -> electrical; ASTM/materials specs -> materials).
- Equipment tags: regex families for ISA-5.1 instruments (e.g., `[A-Z]{1,4}-?\d{3,5}[A-Z]?`), equipment numbers (P-101A, E-2301), line numbers (size-service-number-spec) should be site-specific and validated against the master equipment/tag list from the EDMS/CMMS; VLM-read tags on drawings should be accepted only when they match a known tag or are confirmed by a human (the hallucination evidence in Q1 applies).
- Standards references: extract with a constrained regex-then-LLM step (`(ASME|API|ISO|IEC|ASTM|NFPA|ISA|EN|BS)\s*[A-Z]?\s*[\d.\-]+(?:[-:]\d{4})?`), normalise to a canonical designation plus edition year, and make each standard an entity page in the wiki so clauses citing it link back; edition year matters because the "current revision" logic in Q4 applies to standards too.
- Provenance should be stored at chunk level as `{document_id, revision, page, bbox, section_path}` from the parser's layout output (Docling/MinerU provide page and bounding boxes), so wiki citations can deep-link to a page region.

### Gaps
- No benchmark or paper evaluating LLM classification of engineering document sub-types (spec vs datasheet vs standard vs report) or discipline tagging was found.
- No published, validated regex set for ISA-5.1 tags or standards designations; the ISA standard text itself is paywalled and only secondary blogs were reachable.
- The Cognite thread's actual extraction approach and accuracy were not retrieved (host unreachable).
